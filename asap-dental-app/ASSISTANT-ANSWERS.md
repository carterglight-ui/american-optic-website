## Your visit

### 1. What happens at my first visit?

Your first visit is mostly getting to know you:
- Check in and a quick health history
- X-rays (they take seconds)
- A cleaning if there's time
- The doctor's exam, and a plan explained in plain English

No surprises: we'll tell you what we see and what your options are before anything happens.

### 2. How long does a visit take?

Most checkups and cleanings take 45 to 60 minutes. A first visit can run a little longer because we take X-rays and go over your health history. Treatments vary, and each lesson in Smile School lists the usual time in the chair.

## Feeling nervous

### 14. How can I feel less nervous?

You're in good company. Lots of our patients feel the same way. A few things that help:
- Try the one-minute breathing exercise
- Set up your comfort plan so the team knows what you need
- Bring headphones and your favorite playlist
- Use the stop signal: raise your left hand anytime and we pause

### 15. What sedation options do you have?

We offer three levels:
- Nitrous oxide (laughing gas): you stay awake and relaxed, and it wears off within minutes
- Oral sedation: a pill before your visit to take the edge off
- IV sedation: deeper relaxation for longer procedures like implants or wisdom teeth

The doctor will help you pick what fits you and your treatment.

### 16. Is laughing gas safe?

Nitrous oxide has been used in dentistry for a long time. You breathe it through a small nose mask, you stay awake and can talk, and you just feel calm and a little floaty. It wears off within minutes after the mask comes off, and many people can drive themselves home.

### 17. Do I need a ride after sedation?

For oral or IV sedation, yes. Please plan on a ride home and take it easy the rest of the day. With laughing gas alone, many people can drive themselves once it wears off. The team will tell you for sure before your visit.

### 18. Can I take breaks during treatment?

Absolutely. Raise your left hand anytime and we'll pause. No explaining needed. You can also add "Let me take breaks" to your comfort plan so your team knows ahead of time.

### 19. I have a strong gag reflex. Can you help?

Yes, and you're not alone. Let your team know so they can help:
- Breathe slowly through your nose
- Ask for breaks, and use your stop signal
- Smaller X-ray sensors and numbing gel can help
- Sedation is an option for some people

### 20. It's been years since I saw a dentist.

No judgment here, ever. We see people every day who haven't been in years, and we're just glad you're here. We'll start with a gentle exam, show you what we see, and make a plan at a pace that works for you.

## Symptoms

### 21. I have a toothache. What should I do?

We're sorry you're hurting. We see urgent problems the same day whenever we can. Call [your office] at [office phone].

Until then:
- Rinse with warm salt water
- An over-the-counter pain reliever can help, as directed on the label
- A cold compress on your cheek can ease soreness

If you have swelling in your face, a fever, or trouble swallowing or breathing, get care right away.

### 22. My face or gums are swollen.

Swelling can mean an infection that needs care soon. Call [your office] at [office phone]. If the swelling is spreading, or you have a fever or trouble swallowing or breathing, call 911 or go to the ER.

### 23. My tooth got knocked out!

Act fast. A knocked-out tooth can often be saved within about an hour:
- Pick it up by the top (the crown), not the root
- If it's dirty, rinse it gently. Don't scrub
- Keep it in milk, or tucked in your cheek
- Call [your office] at [office phone].

### 24. I chipped or broke a tooth.

Save any pieces if you can, rinse with warm water, and cover sharp edges with sugar-free gum or dental wax. Small chips can often be smoothed or bonded in one visit. Bigger breaks may need a crown. Call [your office] at [office phone].

### 25. My filling or crown fell out.

Keep the crown if you have it, and avoid chewing on that side. Dental cement from a pharmacy can hold a crown temporarily. Don't use super glue. We can usually fix this quickly. Call [your office] at [office phone].

### 26. My tooth is sensitive to cold or hot.

Sensitivity is common. It can come from worn enamel, gums pulling back, a cavity or a cracked filling.
- Try a sensitivity toothpaste for 2 weeks
- Use a soft brush and gentle pressure

If it lingers, gets worse, or hurts on its own, book a checkup so the doctor can find the cause.

### 27. Why do my gums bleed?

Bleeding gums usually mean they're inflamed from plaque along the gumline. Keep brushing and flossing gently every day. It usually improves in 1 to 2 weeks. If it doesn't, or your gums are pulling back, a deep cleaning may help.

### 28. How do I fix bad breath?

Most bad breath starts in the mouth:
- Brush twice a day, and brush your tongue too
- Floss daily to clear food between teeth
- Drink water, and avoid tobacco

If it sticks around, a cleaning and checkup can rule out gum disease or a cavity.

### 29. My jaw hurts or clicks.

Jaw soreness or clicking can come from clenching, grinding or the jaw joint itself. Soft foods, a warm compress and resting your jaw can help for now. If it keeps up or limits how wide you can open, book a visit so the doctor can take a look.

### 30. I grind my teeth at night.

Grinding can wear teeth down and make your jaw sore. A custom night guard protects your teeth while you sleep. The doctor can check for wear and fit one for you.

### 31. My wisdom tooth hurts.

Wisdom teeth often hurt when there isn't room for them to come in, or when the gum over them gets irritated. Rinse with warm salt water and keep the area clean. If you have swelling or trouble opening your mouth, call us soon. Call [your office] at [office phone].

### 32. I have a sore in my mouth.

Most mouth sores, like canker sores, heal on their own in 1 to 2 weeks. Avoid spicy or acidic foods while they heal. If a sore lasts longer than 2 weeks, keeps coming back, or you notice a lump or patch, book a visit so we can check it.

## Treatments

### 33. How often do I need a cleaning?

Most people do best with a cleaning and checkup every 6 months. It catches small problems while they're still small and easy to fix. Some people with gum disease need cleanings more often.

### 34. What is a deep cleaning?

A deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) cleans below the gumline, where tartar and bacteria hide when gums are inflamed. We numb the area so you're comfortable. It helps your gums heal and tighten back around your teeth.

### 35. Does a filling hurt?

We numb the area first, so you feel pressure and vibration, not pain. Most people say the numbing pinch is the worst part, and it lasts a second. Fillings are tooth-colored and usually done in one visit.

### 36. What is a crown?

A crown is a custom cap that covers and protects a weak or cracked tooth. It usually takes two visits: one to shape the tooth and take an impression, and one to place your finished crown. Ours are made in our own dental lab.

### 37. What is a bridge?

A bridge fills the gap from a missing tooth. A false tooth is held in place by crowns on the teeth beside it. Implants are another option for replacing a tooth, and the doctor can walk you through both.

### 38. Do root canals hurt?

For most people it feels about like a filling. The toothache that brings you in is usually much worse than the treatment, and the treatment is what relieves it. A root canal saves your natural tooth, and a crown usually protects it afterward.

### 39. What happens when a tooth is pulled?

We numb the area so you feel pressure, not sharp pain. The tooth is gently loosened and removed, then we place gauze and go over aftercare. Mild soreness for a few days is normal. We'll also talk about options to replace the tooth if needed.

### 40. Do I need my wisdom teeth out?

Not always. If they come in straight and you can keep them clean, they can stay. Many mouths don't have room, though, and that can cause pain, crowding or infection. A 3D X-ray shows exactly where they sit. Our oral surgery team removes them, with sedation options.

### 41. What is a dental implant?

An implant is a small post placed in the jaw that acts like a tooth root. Once it heals, a custom crown goes on top. It looks, feels and works like a natural tooth, and it doesn't rely on the teeth next to it.

### 42. Can I get an implant the same day?

Sometimes. With an immediate implant, the post can be placed right after a tooth is removed. Whether it's right for you depends on your bone and gums, which the doctor checks with a 3D X-ray.

### 43. What kind of dentures do you offer?

We offer full dentures, partial dentures, overdentures that snap onto implants, and fixed dentures that stay in. Ours are made and adjusted by our own lab team, which helps with fit and quick fixes.

### 44. What is a partial denture?

A partial denture fills gaps when you still have some of your natural teeth. It clips onto the teeth you have and comes out for cleaning.

### 45. Can dentures snap in or stay in place?

Yes. Overdentures snap onto a few implants so they don't slip, and you can still take them out to clean. Fixed dentures are anchored on implants and stay in all the time. The doctor can help you decide which fits you best.

### 46. How does teeth whitening work?

Professional whitening safely lifts stains from coffee, tea and time, with stronger and more even results than store-bought strips. Some people feel mild sensitivity for a day or two. We check that your teeth are healthy enough to whiten first.

### 47. What are veneers?

Veneers are thin, custom shells bonded to the front of your teeth to change their color, shape or size. They're a popular way to refresh a smile. A cosmetic consult is the best place to start.

### 48. Are dental X-rays safe?

Modern digital dental X-rays use very low doses. Our 3D scanner is an open machine with no tunnel: you stand still and bite a guide for a few seconds. Let the team know if you're pregnant or might be.

### 49. Can I see the dentist while pregnant?

Yes. Checkups and cleanings are an important part of a healthy pregnancy, since pregnancy can make gums more sensitive. Just let us know you're pregnant so we can plan X-rays and treatment around it.

## Aftercare

### 50. How do I care for my mouth after an extraction?

For the first few days:
- Bite gently on gauze to stop bleeding
- Skip straws, smoking and spitting hard. They can pull out the healing clot
- Eat soft foods and chew on the other side
- Rinse gently with warm salt water after the first day

### 51. What is dry socket?

Dry socket happens when the blood clot in an extraction site comes out too early, leaving the bone exposed. It usually causes throbbing pain a few days after the extraction. If that happens, call us and we can soothe it quickly. Call [your office] at [office phone].

### 52. When can I eat after a filling?

Wait until the numbness wears off, usually a few hours, so you don't bite your cheek or tongue. Tooth-colored fillings are hard before you leave, so you can chew normally after that. A little sensitivity for a few days is normal.

### 53. What should I expect after a root canal?

The tooth may feel tender for a few days, and over-the-counter pain relief usually helps. Avoid chewing hard foods on that side until your crown is placed, since the tooth is more fragile until then.

### 54. What should I avoid after whitening?

For a couple of days, go easy on coffee, tea, red wine and other staining foods. Some sensitivity is normal and fades within a day or two. Brushing and regular cleanings help keep your smile bright.

### 55. How do I take care of dentures?

- Rinse them after eating
- Brush them daily with a soft brush and denture cleaner, not regular toothpaste
- Soak them overnight so they keep their shape
- Bring them in if they rub or feel loose. Quick adjustments help a lot

## Home care

### 56. How should I brush my teeth?

Brush twice a day for 2 full minutes with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste. Angle the bristles 45° toward the gumline and use gentle circles. Don't forget your tongue. Replace your brush every 3 to 4 months.

### 57. Do I really need to floss?

Yes. Flossing cleans between teeth, where your brush can't reach and where many cavities and gum problems start. Once a day is enough. A water flosser or floss picks work well too, if they make it easier to stick with.

### 58. Is an electric toothbrush better?

Both work well when you brush for 2 minutes with good technique. Many people find an electric brush makes it easier, especially ones with a built-in timer. The best brush is the one you'll use twice a day.

### 59. Should I use mouthwash?

Mouthwash can freshen breath and help a bit with plaque, but it doesn't replace brushing and flossing. A fluoride rinse can help if you get a lot of cavities. Ask your hygienist which one fits you.

### 60. Is fluoride good for my teeth?

Yes. Fluoride strengthens enamel and helps repair the earliest signs of decay. A fluoride toothpaste twice a day is the simplest way to protect your teeth.

### 61. How does sugar and soda affect my teeth?

It's less about how much sugar and more about how often. Each sugary snack or sip feeds bacteria that make acid for about 20 minutes. Have sweets with meals, sip water between, and wait about 30 minutes after soda before you brush.

### 62. How does smoking or vaping affect my teeth?

Smoking and vaping raise your risk of gum disease, stains, bad breath and slower healing after treatment. They also make some procedures, like implants, less likely to succeed. We're happy to help, without judgment, whenever you're ready to cut back.
